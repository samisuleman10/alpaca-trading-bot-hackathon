# How many minutes we could actually have traded an option in

Generated by `scripts/option_bar_coverage.py` over 655 sessions, 2024-01-18 to 2026-08-27,
both ends inclusive.

The share tape has a price for nearly every minute. The option archive
does not, and a minute with no option price is a minute in which the
strategy could form a view and then do nothing about it.

| | Minutes | Share of session time |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Session minutes in the window | 254,370 | 100% |
| With at least one option bar | 252,865 | 99.4% |
| Missing at the edge of a session | 1,223 | 0.5% |
| Missing inside a session | 282 | 0.1% |

**The two kinds of missing minute are not the same thing.**

A minute missing *inside* an otherwise complete session is a quiet
market: nobody traded any of the contracts we were watching. That is a
real condition, the live system would face it too, and the backtest
should count it as a trade it could not make.

A run of missing minutes at the *start or end* of a session, with solid
data in between, is not a market event. On 2024-01-23 every SPY
contract at every expiry stops at 10:48 -- including ones expiring ten
days later, which cannot all have gone quiet in the same instant. That
is Alpaca's archive missing about five hours. The live system would
have seen those minutes; our rehearsal cannot.

**10 of 655 sessions are damaged** -- missing 20 minutes or more, the
strategy's own volume-comparison window, so a hole that size silences
the rule rather than merely thinning it. **5 sessions lose 20 minutes
or more to an edge gap**, which is the archive rather than the market,
and **1 session has no option prices at all**.

## Sessions with no option prices at all

- **2024-02-02** -- the market was open, we have all 390 minutes of SPY's
  share price, and there is not one option bar.

This is the archive, not the market, and it can be checked in one
request: ask for *daily* bars on a contract expiring 2024-02-09 and
the answer skips from 1 February to 5 February. On the 5th that
contract traded 32,891 times. It was not asleep on the 2nd.

These days are unusable rather than thin, and the backtest should
skip them outright rather than record a day on which the strategy
mysteriously never traded.

## What this means for the decision

1. **The trade count is the number under threat, not the profit.** The
   missing minutes are not chosen by whether we would have made money,
   so this does not tilt the result in a direction. It shrinks the
   sample, and the sample was already the binding constraint: the
   pre-committed floor is 150 trades, below which the honest report is
   "underpowered" rather than a profit figure.

2. **The backtest and the live system do not face the same obstacle.**
   Live, the quiet minutes are real and the archive gaps do not exist.
   So the rehearsal understates how many chances the live system gets,
   while overstating nothing. That is the safe direction to be wrong in,
   and it is still a difference that has to be stated rather than
   discovered.

3. **A session this badly holed should not be silently averaged in.**
   Sessions whose edge gap exceeds the damage threshold are recorded
   here by name so that any result computed over them can be recomputed
   without them, and the two answers compared.
